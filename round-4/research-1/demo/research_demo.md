# No Prior Work Separates MT Repair Localization from Iteration

## Summary

This research artifact executes a targeted, logged literature search to determine whether any prior work in machine-translation automatic post-editing (APE) or scoped span editing varies 'breadth of error localization' (a narrow QE-flagged span vs. broadened content-invariant/error detection) and 'iteration/re-verification of a repair' (single-pass vs. checker-guided iterated repair) as two independently varied, separately measured factors -- the same 2x1 design this project's C1 (broad, single-pass) vs. C2 (narrow, iterated) conditions use. ## Research Findings

A targeted, logged literature search (10 verbatim queries across general and scholarly modes, 6 candidate papers fetched and classified) found no published system or ablation study in machine-translation automatic post-editing (APE) or scoped span editing that holds 'breadth of error localization' and 'iteration/re-verification of a repair' apart as two independently varied, separately measured factors. The closest prior work is TEaR (Wang et al., NAACL 2025 Findings / arXiv:2402.16379) [1], which reports an explicit ablation over the number of refinement rounds (1-5, Tables 12-13) but holds its estimation/localization mechanism fixed across every round, and whose own Appendix D experiment on WMT23 Zh-En finds that iterating repeatedly can *hurt* performance relative to a single round -- a one-dimensional (iteration-only) finding with no localization-breadth axis at all. The two closest QE-informed WMT25 Task 3 submissions -- Padmanabhan (2025) [2], already cited in this project's related work, and Sharma (2025) [3], newly surfaced this search -- both use QE-flagged error spans purely as a fixed input signal to a single-pass correction (Sharma's full extracted text contains zero occurrences of 'iterat' anywhere), so neither reaches even the 'varies one factor' bar; localization scope is never itself an ablated variable in either. xTower (Treviso et al., ACL Findings EMNLP 2024 / arXiv:2406.19482) [4], which the planning pass flagged as a possible breadth-then-iteration two-stage design, does not support that reading on inspection: it is architecturally a single explain step consumed once by a downstream corrector (as used by Sharma 2025 [3]), not an isolated two-stage ablation varying breadth and iteration separately -- the related-work section's characterization of xTower should be corrected. Three additional surfaced candidates were ruled out of scope on inspection: SSPO (Sun et al., ACL 2025) [5] iterates DPO *training* rounds, not a test-time detect-repair-verify loop; TranslationCorrect (Wasti, Hung & Lee, ACL 2025 Demo) [6] is a human-in-the-loop annotation/editing tool with no reported ablation; and 'Structure Enables Effective Self-Localization of Errors in LLMs' (Samanta et al., arXiv:2602.02416) [7] is a general LLM self-correction paper with no machine-translation task. Overall, this supports -- and does not require rescoping -- the paper's claim that no prior MT post-editing ablation varies localization breadth and iteration as two independent, separately measured factors, though the claim should be worded specifically about MT automatic post-editing / scoped span editing rather than about self-correction research broadly, since the free scholarly search backend (OpenAlex/Crossref) returned systematically off-topic results for 4 of 5 scholarly-mode queries due to term collisions on words like 'checker,' 'repair,' and 'localization,' limiting how exhaustive this specific search arm could be. One promising lead, 'Smarter edits? Post-editing with error highlights and translation suggestions' (EAMT 2026), was surfaced by search but not yet fetched within this session's time budget and is flagged as the top open item for any follow-up pass.
